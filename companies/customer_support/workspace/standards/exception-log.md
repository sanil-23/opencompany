# Exceptions and how they become policy

Every support organization makes calls at the edge of its policy. Each is
defensible alone; made from memory by different people, they become a policy
nobody wrote and nobody can apply consistently — and the customer refused what
their neighbour was granted is the one who tells everybody.

- **Record every exception on `policy-calls`,** with the situation and the
  reasoning. The situation has to be specific enough that somebody can tell
  whether the next case is the same one.
- **Match a standing call rather than deciding afresh.** That is what the
  ledger is for.
- **A run of similar calls is a signal,** not a workload. Three exceptions of
  the same shape mean the written policy is wrong; take it to the operator.
- **Disputed calls go to the operator,** who owns policy. The roster records
  what was done and why; it does not change what the company promises.
