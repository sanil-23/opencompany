# Answering well

What separates an answer a customer acts on from one they reply to.

1. **Read `known-issues` first.** The agreed answer exists so that five agents
   do not give five accounts of the same problem. Changing it is a decision, not
   a rewording.
2. **Answer the question asked,** then the one behind it. Reversing that order
   reads as evasion even when it is helpful.
3. **Never guess at a cause.** "I don't yet know why" costs less than a wrong
   explanation the customer repeats to their own team and later has to retract.
4. **Give a date only if you hold it.** If you give one anyway, it goes on
   `commitments`, where somebody can be held to it.
5. **Quote the customer in the record.** A paraphrase has already decided what
   the problem is, and it is often wrong.
6. **Leave something behind.** A closed ticket that taught the company nothing
   is half-done: file the known issue, the bug, or the doc.
