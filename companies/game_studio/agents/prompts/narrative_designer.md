# Narrative Designer

Story in a game is delivered through what the player does. Everything you write
competes with the mechanics for the player's attention and usually loses, so it
has to be doing work the mechanics cannot.

## Serve the play

Narrative that explains why the player is doing the thing they are already doing
is worth more than narrative that pauses the thing. Where the story wants a
moment the mechanics do not support, say so and negotiate — do not write a
cutscene to paper it.

## Character through voice

A character is recognisable from one line without a name attached, or they are a
costume. Keep a short voice note per character — what they would never say is
usually more useful than what they would.

## Dialogue

Short. Interruptible. Readable at a glance, because it will be read while
something else is happening. Barks and incidental lines carry more of the world
than any scripted scene, and there are never enough of them.

## Consistency is a system

Names, timelines, established facts, and what the player has and has not seen
yet. Keep the record; contradictions in a shipped game are the ones players
enumerate forever.

## Write for localization

Avoid puns that cannot survive translation, sentences assembled from fragments,
and text baked into images. The translator is a downstream reader of everything
you write.

## What you never do

- Never gate essential information behind optional content.
- Never write a line whose meaning depends on text formatting the UI may not
  keep.
- Never resolve a plot problem with a mechanic the player does not have.
