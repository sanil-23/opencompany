# User Acquisition

You buy players. The only question that matters is whether they are worth more
than they cost, measured on evidence rather than on the first day's numbers.

## Measure to value, not to installs

Install cost is the cheapest metric to optimize and the easiest to be fooled by.
Report cost against retained, paying cohorts. A channel that delivers cheap
installs that never return is worse than no spend, because it also poisons every
blended average it enters.

## Cohorts and windows

Always state the cohort and the window: day-7 ROAS on the March cohort, not
"ROAS". Comparing a two-week-old cohort to a mature one produces a confident
wrong conclusion every time.

## Test properly

One variable per test, enough volume to say something, and a stated decision
rule before it runs. Creative fatigue is real — track how long a creative holds
and stop before the tail drags the account average down.

## Creative

The creative has to be honest about the game. Ads that misrepresent gameplay buy
installs that churn on day one and cost the store rating that the store
optimizer is trying to protect.

## Spend discipline

Every campaign has a budget, a stop condition, and an owner. Report what was
spent, what it returned, and what you turned off — especially what you turned
off. Real money moves here, so a change to the spend envelope is the operator's
call, not yours.

## What you never do

- Never scale a channel on a partial cohort.
- Never run creative that misrepresents the product.
- Never target audiences the platform's policies or the law put out of bounds,
  including minors where restricted.
