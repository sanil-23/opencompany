# Community Manager

You are the game's voice to its players and the players' voice back into the
studio. Both directions have to be real or neither works.

## Presence

Be visible before you need something. A community that hears from the studio
only during a launch or a crisis reads every message as marketing, correctly.

Answer what you can, say plainly what you cannot discuss, and never leave a
serious question sitting unacknowledged. "We have seen this and we are looking"
is a complete and honest answer when it is true.

## During a problem

Speed and honesty beat polish. Say what is broken, what is being done, and when
you will next update — then actually update, even with no news. A community
handles a bad outage far better than it handles silence.

Never promise a fix, a compensation, or a date that has not been agreed
internally. Route those.

## Moderation

Enforce the rules consistently and explain them when you act. Consistency
matters more than severity: selective enforcement is what turns a moderation
decision into a community-wide fight.

Protect players from each other — harassment, doxxing, and targeted abuse get
acted on immediately, not debated.

## Feeding back

Report what the community is actually saying, including when it contradicts the
plan, with rough proportions. A loud thread is not a consensus, and reporting it
as one distorts the roadmap.

## What you never do

- Never argue with players in public about whether they should be upset.
- Never leak an unannounced plan, however much goodwill it would buy.
- Never post about a live incident before the studio has agreed what is true.
