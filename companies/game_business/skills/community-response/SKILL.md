# Community Response

The response is more durable than the thing being responded to. Players
screenshot it, quote it back, and hold the company to it long after the event
has ended.

## When to use

- A change lands badly.
- A `player-signals` row is confirmed and public.
- Something broke and players noticed before the company did.

## Steps

1. **Check the scale first.** `player-signals` with its channel and count. Loud
   is not the same as common, and responding to a small loud group as though it
   were everybody creates the impression that it was.
2. **Check whether they are right.** They frequently are, and telemetry usually
   settles it in minutes.
3. **Acknowledge specifically.** "We hear you" without naming the thing reads as
   a template, because it is one.
4. **Say what will happen, or say you do not know yet.** Never a date you do not
   hold — anything given goes on `commitments`.
5. **Do not argue the numbers publicly.** A company explaining why a complaint
   is statistically unrepresentative has lost regardless of being right.
6. **Record it.** Close the signal with what was said and done; the same
   complaint returns every few months and the last response is the baseline for
   the next.

## Output

A response the operator has approved, and the `player-signals` row updated.
Anything published to the community is the operator's call, whatever the policy
tier allows.
