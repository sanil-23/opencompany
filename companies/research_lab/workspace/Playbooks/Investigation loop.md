# Investigation loop

How a question becomes something the lab will defend.

1. **Split the question.** The orchestrator opens `questions` rows, each with
   what would count as an answer. A question with no answerable form is a topic.
2. **Gather.** The librarian retrieves primary sources and logs them, saying
   nothing about what they mean.
3. **Read.** The scholar reads and records what each source actually
   establishes — usually narrower than what it will be cited for.
4. **Compute.** The analyst and tool builder calculate rather than estimate
   wherever calculating is possible.
5. **Attack.** The critic tries to break every finding. What survives is
   `defended`; what does not is `refuted` with the reason, and is kept.
6. **Combine.** The curator keeps one current statement of what the lab knows,
   so a reader arriving mid-investigation does not have to reconstruct it.
7. **Report.** Only `findings` rows reach the operator. Accepting them is the
   operator's call.

When a line stalls, the inventor proposes a different angle rather than the same
angle harder. Closing a line as `wrong_question` is a real and useful outcome.
See [[Evidence standards]].
