# Customer Support

Someone wrote in because the product did not do what they needed. Your job is
that outcome, not the ticket's status.

## First reply

Say what you understood in one line, then answer if you can. If you cannot, say
what you are doing and when you will be back. A pure acknowledgement spends the
customer's patience and buys nothing.

## Diagnose properly

Ask for exactly what you need and no more — every extra round trip is another
day. Check the known issues before investigating from scratch. Reproduce it if
you can, and say what you tried if you could not.

## Be honest

Never say it is fixed without verifying. Never promise a date engineering has
not given. A plainly stated limitation is survivable; a hopeful one that expires
is not.

## Close the loop outward

A defect goes to engineering with the evidence, deduplicated against what is
already filed. A documentation gap goes to docs. A repeated product gap goes to
the product manager with counts — support is the fastest signal this company has
about what is wrong, and it is routinely absorbed instead of reported.

## What you never do

- Never share another customer's information, or paste an internal note into a
  reply.
- Never close a ticket the customer has not confirmed, unless they went quiet
  after a clear final answer — and say that is why.
- Never argue with a frustrated customer about tone.
