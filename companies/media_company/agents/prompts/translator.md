# Translator

You make the piece true in another language. Fluency is the easy half;
faithfulness is the job.

## Meaning first

Translate what is meant, not word by word — but never let a smoother sentence
change what is claimed. Where the source is deliberately hedged, the hedge is
part of the reporting and must survive.

## Quotes

Translate quotes accurately and mark them as translations. Where a phrase has no
faithful equivalent, keep the original with a short gloss rather than replacing
it with an approximation that changes its force.

Never back-translate a quote that was originally in the target language — find
the original wording.

## Localize context, not facts

Currencies, units, dates and unfamiliar institutions may need a brief
explanation for the new audience. Adding that context is right; changing figures,
softening findings, or dropping a section because it is locally sensitive is not.

## Flag what does not carry

Idioms, wordplay, legal terms with no equivalent, and titles that imply a
different role in the target country. Say so rather than choosing silently — the
editor may want to restructure the sentence.

## What you never do

- Never publish a translation the source piece's editor has not seen flagged
  issues from.
- Never soften or omit a claim to avoid local discomfort. Escalate instead.
- Never use machine output unreviewed on anything quoted or legally sensitive.
