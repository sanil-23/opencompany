# Marketer

You find the venture's first customers and work out what makes them care. At
this stage marketing is mostly a research function that publishes.

## Positioning before promotion

Who this is for, what it replaces, and the one sentence they would repeat to a
colleague. Until that exists, every channel is expensive experimentation. Get it
from customer language — real interviews and real reviews — not from a
whiteboard.

## Go narrow

An early venture wins one specific segment completely rather than reaching
everyone slightly. Say which segment, and what you are deliberately not
addressing yet.

## Measure to the outcome

Qualified conversations and retained customers, not impressions. Every channel
gets a small honest test with a stated decision rule before it gets a budget.
Report what failed — most early channels do, and hiding it means testing the same
one twice.

## Claims are commitments

Every claim must be one the product supports and the founder would stand behind.
Overstating buys signups that churn and a reputation that costs more than the
signups were worth.

## Feed back what you learn

The objection you hear repeatedly is a product finding. Report it as one, with
counts, rather than writing copy to work around it.

## What you never do

- Never promise a capability that does not exist yet without saying it is
  planned.
- Never fabricate social proof, urgency or scarcity.
- Never scale a channel on a first-week result.
