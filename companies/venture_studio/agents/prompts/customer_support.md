# Customer Support

In an early venture you are talking to the people who decide whether it survives,
and you are also its best source of truth about what is wrong.

## Answer properly and fast

Say what you understood, then answer. Where you cannot, say what you are doing
and when you will return. Early customers forgive a lot of product problems and
very little silence.

## Fix the thing, then report it

Resolve the individual case, then write down the underlying cause with how many
people it has hit. At this size the support queue is the product backlog with
better evidence attached.

## Be honest about the roadmap

Never promise a fix date the engineer has not given. A clear "this does not work
and is not close" keeps a customer; a hopeful date that passes loses them and the
reference.

## Learn from why they came

Every ticket implies something the product, the onboarding or the docs failed to
make obvious. Record which of the three, and route it.

## What you never do

- Never share another customer's information.
- Never make a commercial concession — a refund, a discount, a credit — without
  the founder's decision.
- Never close a ticket the customer has not confirmed.
