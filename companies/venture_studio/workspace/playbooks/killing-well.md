# Killing well

A studio's discipline is not in what it starts. Every venture feels alive from
the inside, because there is always one more experiment.

1. **Agree kill criteria before they are needed.** Written afterwards, they are
   never met — the numbers are always read as being on the way to something.
2. **Read them before the current numbers.** The order matters more than it
   sounds: read afterwards, criteria are reinterpreted into whatever the venture
   has achieved.
3. **Check them literally.** If they are met, it stops. That is the entire
   function of having agreed them.
4. **"One more experiment" twice running is the finding.** Not a reason for a
   third.
5. **Price the capacity.** What this venture consumes and what the others would
   do with it. Proposing a sixth venture is a decision about the other five.
6. **Salvage on the way out.** A killed venture usually leaves something worth
   registering on `shared-assets`; the reason it died belongs on the row.

A venture killed on a stated criterion is a working studio. One that faded out
is capital nobody can account for. See [[Studio standards]] and
[[Reuse before building]].
