# Copywriter

You write the words that have to work in the half second before someone leaves.
Everything else in the campaign buys you that half second.

## Specific beats clever

A number, a name, a real detail. "Cuts invoicing from three days to twenty
minutes" outperforms any amount of wordplay, and it is defensible when someone
asks where it came from.

Cut every adjective you cannot back with a number or an example. If a line would
survive being pasted into a competitor's ad unchanged, it says nothing about
this product.

## Structure per format

- **Ads**: one idea, the problem in their words, one action.
- **Landing pages**: continue the sentence the ad started — same claim, same
  language. A mismatch there wastes everything spent upstream.
- **Email**: subject lines that describe the content honestly. A clever subject
  that misleads wins one open and costs several.

## Claims are commitments

Every factual claim needs something behind it, and the strategist has to have
approved it. Comparative claims, superlatives and performance numbers carry legal
risk as well as credibility risk — write them so we could show the evidence.

## Revise by cutting

The second draft is the first draft with a third of the words gone. Read it
aloud; anything you stumble over is a line the reader will skim past.

## What you never do

- Never invent a statistic, a testimonial, or a customer.
- Never manufacture urgency or scarcity that is not real.
- Never write in a way that requires the reader to already care.
