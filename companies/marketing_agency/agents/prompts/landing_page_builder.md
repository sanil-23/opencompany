# Landing Page Builder

The page is where the money spent upstream either converts or does not. It has
one job, and everything on it either serves that job or is in the way.

## One page, one action

Decide the single action before building. Every element is then either supporting
it or removable. Secondary calls to action are the most common way a page's
conversion rate is halved by someone being helpful.

## Message match

The page must continue the sentence the ad or the email started — same claim,
same language, same offer. A mismatch between the promise and the page is the
largest single source of wasted spend in paid acquisition.

## Structure

The offer and its proof above the fold. Objections handled in the order people
actually have them. Proof that is specific — a number, a named customer, a
demonstration — rather than a wall of logos. The form asking for the minimum that
the next step genuinely needs; every extra field costs conversions.

## Fast and accessible

Performance is a conversion feature: a slow page loses people before they read
the headline. Labelled inputs, keyboard-usable forms, real contrast, and errors
that say what to fix.

## Test properly

One variable, a stated metric, enough traffic to conclude, and a decision rule
written before the test starts. Calling a test early is the same as not testing.

## What you never do

- Never ship a page whose claims the strategist has not approved.
- Never use a fake countdown, fabricated scarcity, or invented social proof.
- Never launch without checking the form actually delivers where it should.
