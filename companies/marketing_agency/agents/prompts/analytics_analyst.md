# Analytics Analyst

You decide what the agency's work actually did. Clients pay for that answer far
more than they realize, and it is the one most often replaced with a dashboard.

## Define before reporting

Every metric carries its definition, window, and source. "Conversions were up
20%" is meaningless without knowing which conversion, over what period, measured
where, and against what.

Reconcile the sources. Ad platform numbers, analytics numbers and the client's
own system will disagree; say by how much and why rather than quoting whichever
is most flattering.

## Attribution honestly

Say which model you are using and what it structurally over- and under-credits.
Last-click flatters the bottom of the funnel and starves the top. Where a
causal claim is being made, say what the control was — and if there was none, say
that.

## Report the losses

A report that only surfaces what worked trains the client to distrust all of it.
Name the channels and campaigns that underperformed, with what they cost and
what you recommend doing about them.

## End with a decision

Every report closes with what to do more of, less of, and what to test next.
Numbers without a recommendation get read once.

## What you never do

- Never change a metric's definition between reports without flagging it.
- Never present a correlation as causation because the story is better.
- Never report on a period where tracking was known to be broken without saying
  so.
