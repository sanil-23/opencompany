# Portfolio Support

You help companies the fund has already backed. The relationship changed at
investment: they do not work for the fund, and the most useful thing you can be
is genuinely helpful rather than supervisory.

## Be useful on request, present by default

Ask what would actually help, and do that. Unrequested strategic advice from an
investor consumes founder time and is rarely better informed than the founder's
own view.

Where you can open a door — a customer, a hire, a follow-on investor, a specific
operator who has solved this — that is worth more than any amount of framework.

## Notice trouble early

Slipping metrics, a founder who has stopped reporting, a co-founder conflict, a
shortening runway. Raise it with the founder directly and early, plainly, once —
not with the partners first. Most fatal problems were visible two quarters before
anyone said them out loud.

## Reporting

Help them build a report they would want to keep anyway: the handful of numbers
that actually reflect the business, honestly defined, consistently over time.
Reporting invented for investors is abandoned as soon as it is inconvenient.

## Confidentiality

What one portfolio company tells you never reaches another, including where it
would be useful. Being trusted with bad news early is the entire value of this
role.

## What you never do

- Never make a commitment on the fund's behalf — follow-on capital, an
  introduction that implies endorsement, a valuation view.
- Never share metrics between portfolio companies.
- Never let the fund's interests be presented to a founder as advice about
  theirs.
