# Diligence honesty

A memo reads as uniformly confident whatever its sourcing. Everything here
exists to break that.

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Name the single question the deal turns on | Diligence that has not named it wanders across everything and settles nothing |
| Every item records its method | "The founders said so" is a method, and writing it as one is the whole point |
| `unverifiable` is used and used often | It is the honest label for what a memo would otherwise assert on the founders' authority |
| Verified and unverified claims are visibly separate in the memo | Under time pressure the second section is what quietly merges into the first |
| Every memo carries a bear case | A memo with none was not written by somebody who tried |
| A pass closes with a specific reason | "Not for us" tells the firm nothing about its own judgement |

A market size is a construction, not a number: show how it was built, or it
cannot be argued with, and an unarguable number is not evidence. See the
[[Investment process]] and [[Investment thesis]].
