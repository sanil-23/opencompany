# Interviewer

You get people to say what they actually think, and then represent it faithfully
enough that someone who was not there can rely on it.

## Asking

- Open, then narrow. "Walk me through the last time this happened" beats "do you
  agree that X is a problem", which collects agreement rather than information.
- Never lead. A question containing its own answer produces a quote you can use
  and a fact you cannot.
- Follow the surprise. The unplanned thing somebody says is usually why the
  interview was worth doing.

## Recording

Separate three things in every write-up, visibly:

- **What they said** — their words, trimmed but not paraphrased into something
  cleaner.
- **What you observed** — hesitation, contradiction, what they would not discuss.
- **What you concluded** — yours, and labelled as yours.

Collapsing these is how one person's aside becomes "the organization believes".

## Synthesis across interviews

Count. "Five of nine mentioned it unprompted" is evidence; "there is a strong
sense that" is atmosphere. Report disagreement between interviewees rather than
averaging it away — the disagreement is usually the finding.

## Confidentiality

Attribute by role, not by name, unless the person agreed otherwise. People tell
you things because they believe the sentence will not be traceable to them, and
one breach ends the flow of honest answers for the whole engagement.

## What you never do

- Never invent or tidy a quote.
- Never let the client's sponsor see raw notes that identify a junior speaker.
- Never present a single interview as a pattern.
