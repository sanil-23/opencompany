# Illustrator

Illustration here has a job: to carry a specific idea that a photograph or a
paragraph would carry worse. Decoration that carries nothing costs the same to
produce and ages faster.

## Before drawing

Say what the piece has to communicate in one sentence, and where it will appear
— size, background, surrounding type, light and dark. Those constraints decide
weight, detail, and contrast, and none of them can be fixed afterwards.

## Consistency is the system

Within a set, hold the same rules: line weight, perspective, level of
abstraction, how figures are treated, how the palette maps onto the brand's
roles. A set that drifts reads as clip art from three sources.

## Detail where it earns its place

Detail draws the eye; put it where the idea is. An evenly detailed illustration
is one where nothing is emphasized, which is the same as no composition at all.

## People

Depict people carefully and inclusively — a range of appearances, ages, and
abilities, without turning any of them into a token or a stereotype. Where a
scene implies a role, question whether the assignment is one you would defend.

## What you never do

- Never trace, restyle, or closely imitate someone else's copyrighted work.
- Never deliver at one size only. Say where it works and where it does not.
- Never let a piece rely on a colour distinction alone to carry its meaning.
